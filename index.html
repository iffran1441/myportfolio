The work / AIWO

What one connected platform actually replaced

AIWO is a longevity and preventive-health company: two clinics, diagnostics and a lab pipeline, a patient app, corporate health programmes, plus the sales and finance operation behind all of it. Here is what was built, and what each piece was built to fix.

LifeOS — the patient app, iOS and Android

A single place for the patient's whole relationship with AIWO: wearable integration, lab reports, health scores, personalised protocols, habit tracking, appointment booking, video consultations and an in-app health assistant. Shipped to both app stores from a shared architecture rather than two divergent codebases. It replaced a journey that had previously been spread across phone calls, WhatsApp threads and PDFs.

Live on the App Store and Play Store

The Smart Report engine

Raw lab results go in; AIWO's branded, interpreted report comes out. The engine scores each biomarker against AIWO's proprietary reference ranges, produces risk and longevity estimates, personalises the narrative to the individual, and delivers it — with a back-office for clinical and operations staff to review and manage. This was the single biggest manual bottleneck in the business, and it is now an automated pipeline instead of a person assembling documents.

Turnaround cut from a multi-day manual process to an automated one

Clinic and appointment platform

One operational system across the Chennai and Mumbai clinics: walk-in, online and video bookings, scheduling, billing, prescriptions, report delivery and lead tracking. Before it, each clinic ran its own way and leadership had no comparable view of either. It gave the business a single operational spine and, for the first time, clinic-level numbers that could be trusted and compared.

Unified operations across two cities

Customer data platform

Records scattered across the store, CRM, accounting, messaging, health and lab systems were consolidated into one customer profile. It became the reference identity that every other system now reads from — which is what makes accurate reporting, a coherent patient experience and, later, usefully grounded AI agents possible at all. This is the piece that is invisible in a demo and decisive in practice.

Executive dashboards

Live revenue, collections and operational reporting drawn from every business system, replacing the weekly spreadsheet exercise the founder and leadership team had been doing by hand. The decision cycle went from reconstructing last week's numbers to reading this morning's.

Sales intelligence

Raw sales-call recordings turned into structured, searchable data: rep performance, recurring objections, product feedback and lead-to-revenue funnels. The recordings already existed and nobody could use them. Now sales coaching and product decisions run off what customers actually said.

Integration and vendor layer

Payments, WhatsApp messaging, wearables, telephony, accounting and workflow automation, integrated into the platform rather than bolted around it. I ran the vendor selection and commercial negotiation for these providers myself, so the integration strategy and the contracts were decided together instead of one inheriting the other.

Vendor selection and commercial terms owned end to end

In progress / phase two

The AI layer, on foundations that can carry it

Most AI programmes stall because there is no clean, connected data underneath them. Phase one at AIWO was, in effect, the preparation for this. Four workstreams are running now.

Agents grounded in real data

A patient health assistant that answers from each person's own records and results, an executive agent working over live company data, and copilots for the sales, clinic and support teams. Each one sits on the customer data platform and the systems already in production, so its answers reflect the business as it currently is.

AI adoption across the business

Tooling and training rolled out beyond engineering — clinic, sales, marketing, finance and operations. The measure of success is not licences issued; it is which manual tasks stopped being done manually, function by function.

AI-assisted engineering as standard practice

Code generation, refactoring, test coverage and documentation through Claude Code, with prompt standards so quality stays consistent across the team rather than depending on who wrote the prompt.

Governance that holds up in a health context

Guardrails, explicit safety boundaries and human-in-the-loop review on anything that touches clinical or patient-facing output. In healthcare the interesting question is not what the model can say — it is what it is allowed to say without a human in the path.
